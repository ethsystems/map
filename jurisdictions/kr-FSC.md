## At a Glance

South Korea regulates crypto through anti-money laundering registration and user protection, and is building a tokenised securities market on a permissioned shared ledger. Virtual asset service providers register with the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KoFIU) under the Act on Reporting and Using Specified Financial Transaction Information, and the Virtual Asset User Protection Act, in force since 19 July 2024, requires them to keep users' deposits at banks and users' assets apart from their own. From 20 August 2026 KoFIU also screens major shareholders and requires a debt ratio of 200% or below. Amendments passed on 15 January 2026 recognise a distributed ledger as a legal securities register from 4 February 2027; the draft subordinate rules require that ledger to be shared by at least two account management entities and the Korea Securities Depository, with no direct fee for using it. South Korea has no law for stablecoin issuers; the government's review draft of the Digital Asset Basic Act discussed on 4 March 2026 has banks owning 50% plus one of won stablecoin issuers. Project Hangang tests bank deposit tokens on the Bank of Korea's CBDC system, with phase 2 approved on 15 July 2026. The point that matters for privacy engineering is the reach of transfer reporting. The Enforcement Decree approved on 11 August 2026 extends the travel rule from transfers of KRW 1 million or more to transfers of any size between registered providers, six months after promulgation, and requires a KoFIU report for each transfer of KRW 10 million or more to overseas providers and wallet service providers. A draft Foreign Exchange Transactions Act decree, published on 7 October 2026 and due in force on 3 December 2026, would make providers register for transfers to overseas providers and to personal wallets and report each one to the Bank of Korea's foreign exchange network.

## What to Watch

- The final text of the foreign exchange decree, which would bring transfers to self-hosted wallets under per-transfer reporting to the central bank.
- Whether the Digital Asset Basic Act reaches the National Assembly, and whether the bank-majority stablecoin issuer model survives it.
- The tokenised securities start on 4 February 2027: phase 1 covers private money market funds and bonds for institutional investors, trust-held unlisted stocks and fractional investment securities, and phase 3 seeks onchain payment linked to stablecoins.
- Whether Project Hangang deposit tokens move beyond a test under innovative financial service designations.
