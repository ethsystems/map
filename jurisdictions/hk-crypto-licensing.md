## At a Glance

Under "One Country, Two Systems," Hong Kong runs a separate, progressive regime, distinct from the mainland ban. Since 2023 it has offered a licensing path (the SFC's VASP regime, operated through virtual asset trading platform licences) covering exchanges, custody, and professional asset management, positioning itself as an APAC institutional crypto hub. It is permissive but licensed: retail access is limited to SFC-approved tokens, with broader scope for professional investors. Stablecoins belong to a different regulator: the Stablecoins Ordinance (Cap. 656) commenced on 1 August 2025 and makes issuing a fiat-referenced stablecoin a licensed activity supervised by the HKMA. The HKMA granted the first two issuer licences on 10 April 2026, from 36 applications received by the 30 September 2025 deadline, and the register still lists those two. The licensing perimeter is widening: the Virtual Asset Services Legislation (Amendment) Bill 2026, gazetted on 20 November 2026, would require an SFC licence or registration for virtual asset dealing, advisory, management and custody services, with HKMA-regulated banks and stored value facility licensees registering with the SFC, and SFC consultation conclusions of 6 November 2026 restrict names such as "crypto exchange" to SFC-approved firms. On 26 October 2026 the SFC approved the first secondary trading of tokenised SFC-authorised money market funds on a licensed platform. The point that matters for privacy engineering is reporting: the Legislative Council passed the Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) bill on 4 November 2026, so service providers collect user and transaction information from 2027 and the Inland Revenue Department exchanges it with partner jurisdictions from 2028.

## What to Watch

- The Virtual Asset Services Bill, due for First Reading on 2 December 2026, and the transition for dealers, custodians, advisers and managers now operating outside a licence.
- How quickly the HKMA widens the licensed-issuer set beyond the first two.
- Whether the SFC widens secondary trading of tokenised funds beyond money market funds.
- Capital-flow boundaries between Hong Kong and mainland China.
