## At a Glance

Japan is permissive and registration-based, and is moving crypto-asset trading from payments law into securities law. Crypto-asset exchange service providers have registered with the Financial Services Agency (FSA) under the Payment Services Act since April 2017, must segregate and audit customer assets, and may list only tokens reviewed by a self-regulatory organisation. Act No. 64 of 2026, passed on 15 July and promulgated on 23 July 2026, moves crypto-asset trading to the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act (FIEA), adds disclosure duties for token issuers and prohibits insider trading in crypto-assets; its crypto provisions start on a date set by cabinet order within one year of promulgation. Stablecoins redeemable at par are electronic payment instruments, issued only by banks, funds transfer service providers or trust companies. Rules in force since 1 June 2026 let trust-type stablecoins hold part of their backing in government bonds of three months or less and term deposits, and treat a stablecoin issued through a foreign trust as an electronic payment instrument where the foreign law is equivalent. Security tokens have been "electronically recorded transferable rights" under the FIEA since May 2020. The point that matters for privacy engineering is that the travel rule, in effect since June 2023 with no threshold stated in the FSA summary, attaches the originator's and beneficiary's name, address or customer number and blockchain address to every provider-to-provider transfer, and the FSA and National Police Agency asked exchanges on 6 August 2026 to pre-register and screen withdrawal destinations and to hold withdrawals to new ones.

## What to Watch

- The cabinet order that sets the start date of the FIEA crypto provisions, and the Cabinet Office Orders that set capital, disclosure and insider-trading standards.
- The fiscal 2027 tax reform request to exempt trust-type stablecoins from the trustee filings tax law requires on each change of beneficiary, which a trustee cannot make for a token that passes between unknown holders.
- Whether the withdrawal-destination measures, an anti-fraud request to the self-regulatory association that does not distinguish another exchange from a self-hosted wallet, become a supervisory requirement.
- The Bank of Japan's CBDC forum, reorganised in 2026 with a focus that also serves wholesale payments and tokenisation after the pilot found no technical knockout factor.
