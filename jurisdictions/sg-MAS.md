## At a Glance

Singapore is permissive toward digital asset activity and heavily licensed in how it permits it. Digital payment token services fall under the Payment Services Act 2019, and since 30 June 2025 a separate Digital Token Service Provider regime under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2022 has captured Singapore-incorporated firms serving customers wholly outside Singapore. That regime commenced without a transition window, and MAS indicated it would rarely license firms whose substantive activity sits entirely abroad, because it cannot supervise them. The stablecoin position is easy to overstate: MAS finalised the policy features of its single-currency stablecoin framework in August 2023, covering tokens pegged to the Singapore dollar or a G10 currency, but the implementing legislation has not yet been passed, so the "MAS-regulated stablecoin" label has no statutory basis yet. MAS consulted on the implementing amendments to the Payment Services Act 2019 from 1 September to 16 October 2026, with subsidiary legislation to be consulted on separately; a bill has still to reach Parliament. Wholesale settlement is moving from trials to operation: MAS issued S$100 million of tokenised MAS Bills on its wholesale central bank digital currency network in November 2026, and said on 19 November 2026 that the network, the SGD Testnet, will become an operational utility named SG Digital Ledger. For privacy engineering, Project Simbolon, published by MAS and the Deutsche Bundesbank on 16 November 2026, settled EUR/SGD payment-versus-payment across two separate wholesale CBDC test ledgers using zero-knowledge proofs instead of a shared ledger, and found that legal finality and the governance of relayers and provers would need to be settled before wider use. From 1 January 2027 crypto-asset service providers with a Singapore nexus carry out CARF due diligence on users and file annual returns, with exchanges with partner jurisdictions from September 2028.

## What to Watch

- The bill that follows the September 2026 consultation, which determines when the single-currency framework acquires legal force.
- Treatment of tokens that fall outside the single-currency scope, including foreign-issued and multi-currency stablecoins, which stay under the general digital payment token rules.
- Travel rule application to transfers involving unhosted wallets.
- SG Digital Ledger's live trials in 2027, and BLOOM, which Bank Negara Malaysia and the Bank of Thailand joined on 23 October 2026, on settlement in tokenised bank deposits and regulated stablecoins across borders.
